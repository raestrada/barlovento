# The Armada of Acero (steel)

> Principle: **Body**. Faction: **The Armada of Acero** — a hard-science space
> navy in the spirit of *Battlestar Galactica* / *Star Wars*. Doctrine: mass,
> formations, and the line of battle.

This is the deep-dive for the faction summarized in `../DESIGN.md` §7.1.

---

## 1. Summary

The Acero are the old power of the Star Sea: a militarized industrial state
that rose to interstellar scale on **cheap, abundant dark energy** — the
éteron, their "spice". They do not worship the wind and they do not need it to
move; they *burn* it. When the Calm began to kill the wind, it did not strand
their ships — it began to starve their civilization.

## 2. History: the éteron boom

- Origin: a harsh industrial homeworld, **Anvil**, where the discovery of the
  **éteron** — the particle of the Éther field — turned dark energy into a
  commodity. Éteron was abundant, clean, and almost free once extraction was
  industrialized.
- The Acero did not become interstellar out of necessity or belief; they did it
  because **energy had stopped being a constraint**. Industry, FTL
  communication, life support, shipbuilding — all ran on éteron.
- Over centuries they expanded into a network of colonies bound by energy
  logistics: **éteron wells**, extraction rigs, and the fortified shipping
  lanes that carried refined éteron back to the Forge worlds.
- The navy grew because the navy **protected the wells**. Over time, the fleet
  and the state became the same institution.

## 3. Society and government

- The Acero are a **military-industrial state**. Their government *is* the
  navy: admirals sit where ministers would elsewhere. Rank is meritocratic and
  engineering is the highest prestige — an engineer who keeps a fleet alive is
  honored like a war hero.
- Culture is disciplined and sacrificial: **"Steel endures."** They value the
  body, the material, the thing that holds. They have little patience for the
  Vinculados' mysticism or the Free Tide's romance — to them the wind is a
  *resource*, and treating it as sacred is the luxury of the weak.
- Ideological core: the sea is not alive; it is **fuel**. The correct response
  to a dying sea is to engineer around it, not to pray to it.

## 4. The éteron economy ("the spice")

- Éteron is refined from the Éther field at **wells** — regions of high field
  gradient. Refining is energy-hungry but the return is enormous; one working
  well can power a star system.
- The whole Acero economy is a **logistics spine**: wells → refineries → fleet
  → colonies. Disrupt any link and the system begins to fail.
- This is the Dune parallel made material: **control of the wells is control of
  the Concord**. The Calm, by killing the gradient at the front, shuts wells
  down one by one.
- **The regression threat:** without éteron, Acero ships still fly (reaction
  drives, fusion), but the civilization's high-tech economy does not. The
  worst case is collapse back to a **pre-interstellar stage** — a slow, orderly
  empire turning into scattered colonies.

## 5. Technology and propulsion

- **Propulsion:** Newtonian and relativistic. Fusion and antimatter reaction
  drives, mass and momentum — no wind required. This is their independence,
  and their pride.
- **Éteron tech:** used for power, not motion — FTL comms, energy grids,
  life support, shipbuilding, and the **Sluice** engines that can locally shape
  or slow the field.
- **Weakness:** reaction drives are *expensive and heavy* compared to sailing
  the wind. Acero ships are mass-dense, slow to turn, and greedy for the very
  resource that is vanishing.

## 6. Doctrine: mass and the line of battle

- The Acero fight as a **wall of steel**: capitals in a line of battle,
  escorts screening, strike craft ranging ahead. Formations are not cosmetic —
  they are the faction's core mechanic.
- **Armor by facing:** bows are heavily armored; flanks and stern are thinner.
  Acero ships are built to fight head-on, to **cross the T**, and to rotate
  damaged facings out of the enemy's arc — exactly like sail-era line tactics.
- **Inertia profile:** the highest mass and the slowest turns in the game. An
  Acero ship cannot dodge; it must **anticipate**. Where a Free Tide corsair
  reacts and a Vinculados ace duels, an Acero captain *commits*.

### Blurbs

- **Anvil-class Dreadnought** — the heaviest thing in the fleet and the
  slowest to turn. It does not maneuver; it *defines* where the line is.
- **Hammer-class Battlecruiser** — the head of the line, built to smash a hole
  with forward mass-drivers, then roll away before the enemy answers.
- **Foundry-class Carrier** — the force-projector; its strike wings extend
  reach far beyond its own guns.
- **Crucible-class Cruiser** — the anonymous backbone; most fleets are mostly
  Crucibles, and most battles are won or lost on how they are placed.
- **Dirk-class Frigate** — screens capitals with flak and point defense; the
  fleet's immune system.
- **Chisel-class Corvette** — the fastest Acero hull, used to scout, bait, and
  harry flanks, not to hold a line.

## 8. Hero types (the aces)

The player's 2-4 hero squad, in Acero form, are officers and pilots of the
fleet:

| Hero | Archetype | Mass | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fleet Admiral** | Flagship command | Capital (flagship) | Fleet-wide auras: formation, initiative, repair |
| **Strike Ace** | Elite pilot | Strike craft | High-skill interception, weak-point attacks |
| **Breaker Captain** | Assault frigate | Mid-mass | Armor-cracker, aggressive boarder/duelist |

- **Fleet Admiral** — commands from a capital; slow, but their abilities shape
  the whole fleet (formation bonuses, re-targeting, emergency repairs). The
  embodiment of the "plan, don't react" doctrine.
- **Strike Ace** — the Viper/X-wing fantasy. Low mass, high ceiling; dances
  through escorts and knifes at the weak stern of a capital. Cannot kill a
  capital alone, but can open the hole for the fleet.
- **Breaker Captain** — commands a specialized assault frigate: the
  armor-breaker. Mid-mass, mid-agility; the aggressive tempo hero who turns a
  broadside into a rout.

## 9. Gameplay identity (mechanics)

- **Admiral phase:** Acero plans formations and approach; they gain
  **initiative from formation coherence** (the better the line, the stronger
  the opening). They are the strongest Admiral-phase faction because their
  whole doctrine is pre-battle positioning.
- **Ace phase:** sluggish turns and heavy inertia mean an Acero player commits
  each move early; the skill is *anticipation*, not reaction. Strong bow armor
  rewards facing discipline; a broadside from an exposed flank is catastrophic.
- **Resource:** **éteron**, spent and stockpiled. Income scales with the
  distance from the Calm front — every lost well is a budget cut.
- **Asymmetry in the crisis:** the Calm never stops Acero ships, only their
  economy. So while the Free Tide is becalmed and the Vinculados go cold, the
  Acero keep sailing — and look the guiltiest for it.

## 10. The Calm and the war goal

- **The Sluice** — Acero's signature infrastructure: massive engine-stations
  that locally shape the Éther field, slowing or redirecting the Calm's front.
  They cannot stop it, only buy time and steer where it bites.
- **Harvester fleets** secure the remaining wells; **fortified lanes** carry
  éteron home. The strategic layer for Acero is an engineering problem: how to
  wall off the dying sea long enough to keep the lights on.
- **Victory (war goal):** contain the Calm and **domesticate the Éther** — a
  controlled, engineered sea under the Concord. It is a vision of the universe
  as a machine that keeps running, at any cost.

## 11. Visual identity

Angular, brutalist, massed. Dark gunmetal, riveted plate, ember-orange
thrusters. Heavy capital ships with escort fighters; scale references of
gantries and crew for mass. See
[`../art/prompts/factions/acero.md`](../art/prompts/factions/acero.md) for the
canonical prompt tokens.

## 12. Mood and audio

Disciplined, industrial, militaristic. The sound of a fleet is the hum of
massive drives and the bark of broadsides; the score is brass and percussion —
order, not melody.
