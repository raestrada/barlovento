# The Free Tide (wind)

> Principle: **Spirit**. Faction: **The Free Tide** (*La Marea Libre*) —
> steampunk corsairs who sail the star-sea, in the spirit of the Sankuokai
> sailing-ship spacecraft and Miyazaki's *Porco Rosso*. Doctrine: master the
> wind.

This is the deep-dive for the faction summarized in `../DESIGN.md` §7.3.

---

## 1. Summary

The Free Tide are the romantics and the nomads: a people who never settled a
world and never needed an engine. They sail the Éther directly, on sealed
timber hulls and sail membranes that catch the wind of the Star Sea. They are
the only
faction that does not *use* the sea so much as **live on it** — and they are
the first to die when the wind dies.

## 2. History: the worldless

- Origin: the Free Tide have no homeworld. They are the descendants of exiles,
  castaways and dreamers who took to the sea between the stars and never came
  back down.
- Where the Acero engineered their way into space and the Vinculados *heard*
  it, the Free Tide simply **sailed it**: artisanal, empirical, learned by hand
  and passed down in song, not in manuals.
- Their knowledge is oral and personal — every ship is built by its crew, every
  sail cut by the captain who will fly it. There is no fleet doctrine because
  there is no admiralty; there is only the tide and those who ride it.

## 3. Society and government

- A loose confederation of **captains**, not a state. Authority is earned and
  temporary; a captain leads while the crew follows, and no longer.
- Culture is freedom, craft and romance: sea shanties, oath-bound crews,
  rivalries settled by race, not war. They worship nothing in the abstract —
  the **wind itself** is their faith, because it is their breath.
- Etymology of their faith: Greek *pneuma* and Hebrew *ruah* mean both **wind**
  and **spirit**. To the Free Tide the sea is literally the universe's breath.
  When the wind dies, the spirit dies with it.

## 4. The craft: riding the wind

- Their ships are sealed timber galleons and brigantines fitted with **éther
  sails** — large semi-rigid membrane panels that couple to the field's
  gradients, turning dark-energy currents into thrust. The sails must be vast:
  the gradient is faint, so sail area is thrust.
- Banks of **éther-oars** — articulated field-vanes along the flanks — sweep
  the sea for maneuvering and for emergency thrust when the sails cannot catch
  a becalmed sky. A galley that rows the void.
- They do not fight inertia; they **ride** it. A Free Tide ship tacks, trims
  and lets the sea carry it. What is an obstacle to an Acero captain is a
  resource to a Windrider.
- Auxiliary power is desperate and weak: small steam boilers give a trickle of
  emergency thrust when becalmed. It is enough to limp, never to fight.

## 5. Technology

- Treated timber, brass, riveted copper, glass viewports, hand-cut sail
  membranes. Everything is built, not manufactured — no two ships are alike.
  Hulls are sealed and pressurized for the vacuum; life glows warm amber
  inside.
- **Sails:** the core technology, coupling directly to the field. They are
  large membrane panels on radial booms; their arrangement determines speed,
  turning, and how well a ship rides a current.
- **Oars:** banks of articulated field-vanes that sweep the éther for
  maneuvering and emergency thrust when becalmed — the galley's answer to a
  dead sky.
- **Weapons:** broadsides of brass-cased mass-drivers firing shot and magnetic
  grapples, plus tethers for boarding. Close, personal, and a little unfair —
  exactly the corsair way.

## 6. Doctrine: master the wind

- The Free Tide fight the **sea** as much as the enemy. Currents are terrain:
  windward is life, leeward is death. A captain who reads the wind controls the
  fight before a shot is fired.
- **Inertia profile:** medium mass, but the most *drift* in the game — and the
  only faction for whom drift is an asset. They accelerate with the wind and
  coast with it; their maneuvers are slow, sweeping arcs rather than sharp
  turns.
- **Becalming** is their unique nightmare: no gradient means no sail thrust.
  A becalmed ship is dead in the water.

### Blurbs

- **Windjammer** — the great many-boomed ship, home and hearth and weapon at
  once. A captain's Windjammer is their soul made timber and sail.
- **Galleon** — the weight of the tide: slow, broad, bristling with brass
  mass-drivers, built to trade broadsides like an old warship.
- **Brigantine** — the raider: sweeps in on the wind, strikes, and is gone
  before the smoke clears.
- **Barque** — the honest workhorse; most of the tide is made of Barques.
- **Caravel** — the far-eyed scout; goes where the others cannot yet see.
- **Skiff** — a launch for boarding and skirmish; cheap, brave, and short-lived.

## 8. Hero types (the aces)

| Hero | Archetype | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Windrider Captain** | Helmsman | Rides currents, grants drift/flow bonuses |
| **Harpooner** | Boarding master | Close-range assault, grapples, boarding |
| **Windcaller** | Shaman | Briefly re-awakens the wind, buffs the sea |

- **Windrider Captain** — the corsair ideal: reads the wind, holds the helm,
  and turns a current into a killing run. Grants movement and drift to the
  whole squad.
- **Harpooner** — the brawler of the tide: grapples a target, boards it, and
  wins fights at knife-range where armor counts for nothing.
- **Windcaller** — the mystic who *speaks* to the sea. Where the Calm has
  killed the wind, a Windcaller can briefly call it back — a tiny, precious
  miracle that no other faction can perform.

## 9. Gameplay identity (mechanics)

- **Admiral phase:** the strongest *readers* of the map. Currents are visible
  to them (and less so to others), so their approach is about ambush along the
  wind and position relative to leeward.
- **Ace phase:** mid-power, but defined by **flow** — chaining movement along
  currents and turning drift into advantage. They cannot duel like the
  Vinculados or absorb like the Acero; they *dance* with the sea.
- **Currents as terrain:** their core mechanic. Windward grants speed; leeward
  robs it. The Windcaller can locally re-awaken dead current for a few turns.
- **Resource:** the **wind itself**. They extract nothing; their "economy" is
  the availability of current. As the Calm advances, their mobility — their
  whole way of life — thins with it.

## 10. The Calm and the war goal

- The Calm is death to the Free Tide in a way it is not to the others: a
  becalmed ship is **immobile**, not merely poorer or colder. They face
  extinction first.
- They believe the wind is not dying naturally — it is being **killed**: stolen
  by the Acero's greed and punctured by the Vinculados' hunger. Their war is
  righteous in their own eyes.
- The hidden shame: the Free Tide over-sailed the winds themselves, thinning
  the local currents until the fabric tore. The ones who loved the sea most may
  have loved it to death.
- **Victory (war goal):** find the **source of the wind** and **re-awaken it** —
  restore the sea, and with it the breath and spirit of every living thing that
  sails it.

## 11. Visual identity

Sealed sailing-ship hulls in space — galleons and brigantines re-engineered
for vacuum. Treated timber, brass, glass viewports, and semi-rigid cyan sail
membranes on radial booms, with warm amber hab-light inside. The icon is a
*spaceworthy* galleon, not a metal rocket and not a literal boat. See
[`../art/prompts/factions/marea-libre.md`](../art/prompts/factions/marea-libre.md)
for the canonical prompt tokens.

## 12. Mood and audio

Romantic, weathered, adventurous. The sound of the Free Tide is the creak of
timber and the faint hum of sail membranes under starlight, with a shanty
humming under the wind. Hope, carried on a dying breeze.
